Hooke's Law — Short Explanation

Hooke’s Law states that, within the elastic limit of a material, the force required to stretch or compress a spring is proportional to the displacement produced. Mathematically, it is written as:

Formula:

F = -k · x

  • F: Force applied to the spring (Newtons, N).
  • k: Spring constant (N/m), which measures stiffness.
  • x: Displacement or deformation (meters, m).

This principle is widely used in engineering and design: suspension systems, watches, weighing scales, and even in earthquake-resistant structures. It helps us understand how materials store and release energy when they are stretched or compressed.

Hooke’s Law continues to be a key concept in physics, showing the close connection between mathematics, science, and real-world applications.
